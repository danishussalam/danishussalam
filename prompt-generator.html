FAQ

Common Questions

What is prompt engineering and why does it matter for researchers?

Prompt engineering is the practice of crafting inputs to AI systems to get better outputs. For researchers, well-structured prompts can improve the quality of literature reviews, data analysis, writing assistance, and ideation—without needing to learn to code or use complex APIs.

How is this different from just asking ChatGPT directly?

Unstructured prompts often produce inconsistent, meandering, or off-target responses. Frameworks enforce discipline: they ensure your prompt includes a clear role, context, constraints, and output format. The result is faster, more focused AI assistance aligned with your actual needs.

Which framework should I use for academic writing tasks?

Start with CO-STAR to define context, audience, and tone. Then layer in Few-Shot to show examples of the writing style you want. For structural advice, use RISE. For logic-heavy analysis, combine CoT and ROSE.

Can I use AI-generated prompts for my published research?

This tool generates prompts, not research outputs. The prompts themselves are not your intellectual property—they're just templates. What you do with them (feed them to AI, use the outputs in analysis, etc.) is your responsibility. Always review AI outputs critically, cite appropriately, and follow your institution's policies on AI use in research.

Is my input stored or shared?

Your input is sent to Groq's API (not OpenAI's) to generate refined prompts. We track a simple counter of total tool uses, but we do not log, store, or analyse the content of your prompts. Do not enter sensitive data. For sensitive work, run the tool locally or behind a VPN.

What if the output doesn't look right?

Refine your input. Be more specific about what you want: add examples of the tone you need, clarify the audience, or specify constraints. Then regenerate. You can also try a different framework—some frameworks work better for some tasks than others. The Hybrid option intelligently combines frameworks; try that if a single framework isn't clicking.
